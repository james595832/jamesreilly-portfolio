<TextBlock title="What I was asked to do">
  Payroc was fully remote and still felt like a startup. Several companies had merged. There was an appetite to grow, and no design capability. The business is card processing, in the same space as Stripe, Adyen, and J.P. Morgan.

  I was hired as design manager when the design team was me. Polishing the marketing site and hiring later would have left every product with a different face. I hired, put a design system under the work, and then shipped the things that had to share it.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="What I did">
  The public site had to read as one company, on a laptop, a tablet, and a phone. The old site did a job, and it looked like a smaller firm next to the products we were about to build. A reskin would have been faster. It would also have kept an information architecture that could not take more products. The new site has a clearer product story and room to add to it, so sales could send the link without apologising for it.

  Partner Engage was the largest product: partners and merchants managing a portfolio, with reporting and onboarding. The existing platform worked, and it felt like a merger. Dense navigation, and reporting you had to decode. I did not start in Figma. I ran discovery with sales, product, and leadership: what they were trying to do, where the current tool failed, and what we could measure. The redesign is that list. A partner can see the book of merchants and move through it. Navigation, reporting, and onboarding follow how they work, not how the old menus were filed.

  Support was becoming a queue of how-to calls. I designed a support hub in the same direction as Partner Engage, so a customer does not feel they have left the company to find an answer. Integrators were a different audience. The marketing site was never going to serve them. The developer portal holds the APIs, onboarding, and reference.

  Those four surfaces only stay related if the components, patterns, and brand rules are shared. The design system was not a file I made at the end. It is what let a new team ship a year of product without four visual languages.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="How I worked">
  Leadership would list the problems. I came back with a scoped proposal, researched and prototyped it, took it back, and then it went to engineering. The teams were small, so I could talk to the people who could say yes. At that table the useful sentence was about the operation, not about the craft. Fewer calls, a partner who can read their own book, an integrator who can find the API.

  What you see is my direction, carried by very good designers. I left because I was not designing enough myself. I went back to doing the work.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="What came of it">
  In twelve months: a design team, a design system, the enterprise website, developer documentation, a support hub, and Partner Engage.
</TextBlock>
