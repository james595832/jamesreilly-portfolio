<TextBlock>
  Aced-It helps designers get organised for interviews. Practise the questions, do whiteboard challenges, and keep the answers you would give. I built it for myself first. I wanted to speak an answer out loud and stick to the points, instead of waffling.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="Why I built it">
  I spoke to designer friends. What everyone hates is trying to organise their experience into an answer, and the prep around that. The companies I looked at serve developers. Designers were missing the chance to practise the questions they actually get.

  My role is everything. The design, the code, the architecture, and the logic.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="What I started with">
  I started with questions, the ability to speak your answer, and a grade from the AI. The grade uses an answer bank. It looks for whether you hit the points it is looking for, and it scores you against that.

  The research changed the plan. A design interview is not one conversation. It is a screen, then a meeting with product, an engineer, and a designer, then a whiteboard, then the CEO. I changed the roadmap so the product follows that, instead of a developer test with the word design swapped in.

  I had also planned a portfolio review. Scanning a portfolio site is difficult to get right, so that is not the route. What is coming is a template. You fill in your answers, they stay on file, and later the questions can use them.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="Phase 1">
  I had grand ideas of shipping three or four of those features together. Then I realised I was building it on my own. I needed to go feature by feature. Phase 1 is the questions and the grade. You upload your CV, the job description, and the company URL, and the platform writes questions you would actually face.

  The one I will stand behind is “why do you want to work here?”. The URL is used to read the company site, and then you are tested on whether you have done that research. That is a realistic interview, not a generic bank of questions.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="The whiteboard">
  I had a working version of the whiteboard. You can ask questions, the way you would in the room, and get some direction. There is a timer, and the outcome is graded.

  It is not released. Drawing on a MacBook or a phone is tricky, and that is where it needs the work. The challenge should be easy to do. You should not be held back by a clunky build.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="Where it is">
  What is live in phase 1 is the landing page, the subscription, and the job upload. It has had a soft launch with designer friends, to iron out bugs. Nobody is paying yet. I am preparing the marketing launch before the end of the year. It is at <a href="https://acedit.app" class="case-inline-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">acedit.app</a>.
</TextBlock>
