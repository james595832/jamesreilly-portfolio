<TextBlock>
  I was brought in as a freelance designer to redesign NatWest online banking. People come on to check a balance, then do a task. The account table on the old interface was all right. The rest was not clear. Pay someone, or say you had lost a card, was not easy to find.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="Why are they in the branch?">
  A branch visit is usually a pain. You spend twenty minutes getting through to the right person, and ten minutes later you are told to come in. I wanted to hear why they had to come in, and then ask the bank why we could not offer it online.

  Paying in a cheque, and a change of address, were two of those. I designed them so they could be done with the camera on your device. Before you update your personal address, you use your online banking credentials. That was the extra security.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="Cards, not a table">
  A table makes more sense in business banking, where someone might have a hundred accounts. In consumer banking the average is about three. Cards let you look at each balance on its own, instead of scanning a table from top to bottom and then left to right.

  I also thought we could put extra information on the card, such as the last five transactions. Testing said no. Customers were happy to click through once and see all of the transactions, rather than only five.

  We did that in sessions, one card at a time, until we had enough feedback to move on and build up the interface.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="The £1 check">
  Pay on the dashboard was part of making the task easy to find. The £1 check was my idea. I transferred my rent to an old landlord and never got it back. From that day I have always sent £1 first, to be sure the details are right.

  I took it to fraud and compliance. It needed to be written clearly, so people understood why. Customers were positive about it. On the first payment you send £1. If they confirm it is correct, and they are a confirmed payee, you are free to send more.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="The watch">
  The other feature that went with this was the Apple Watch. If you lose the card, or forget it, a code is sent to the watch and you can take money out at a cash machine.
</TextBlock>

<TextBlock title="What went live">
  The project was beset by internal problems. Leadership changed more than once. The technology team downed tools mid-project to change the stack. The designers kept going.

  In the end the account interface went live with the cards, quick payments, and the ability to add further products. I think the clearer interface worked. It was easier to follow and understand, especially for older customers, who felt it was more inclusive.

  When you signed up, we kept that information on file, so you did not have to keep applying. If nothing in your circumstances had changed, and we do ask, you could get to other products without starting the paperwork again. That was a real friction. Why all the paper?
</TextBlock>
